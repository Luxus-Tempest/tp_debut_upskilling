# CHAPTER 85 

## MACHINES, APPARATUS AND ELECTRICAL EQUIPMENT AND THEIR PARTS; SOUND RECORDING OR REPRODUCTION APPARATUS, IMAGE AND SOUND RECORDING OR REPRODUCTION APPARATUS FOR TELEVISION, AND PARTS AND ACCESSORIES OF THESE DEVICES

## Notes.

1.- Excluded from this Chapter are:
a) electrically heated blankets, cushions, sleeping bags and similar articles; electrically heated clothing, shoes, ear warmers and other articles worn on the person;
b) glass products of  $n^{\circ} 70.11$;
c) machines and apparatus of $n^{\circ} 84.86$;
d) vacuum cleaners of the types used in medicine, surgery, or for dental or veterinary practice ($n^{\circ} 90.18$);
e) electrically heated furniture of Chapter 94.
2.- Articles that could fall under $n^{\circ}s 85.01 to 85.04$, on the one hand, and under $n^{\circ}s 85.11, 85.12, 85.40, 85.41$, or 85.42, on the other hand, are classified in the last five positions of these.

However, mercury steam mutators with metallic tanks are included in $n^{\circ} 85.04$.
3.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ} 85.07$, the term electric accumulators also includes accumulators presented with auxiliary elements that contribute to the storage and delivery function performed by the accumulator or that are designed to protect the latter from potential damage, such as electrical connectors, temperature control devices (such as thermistors), and circuit protection devices. They may also include part of the protective envelope of the devices to which they are intended.
4.- $n^{\circ} 85.09$ covers, provided that they are electromechanical devices commonly used for domestic purposes:
a) parquet polishers, food grinders and mixers, fruit and vegetable presses, of any weight;
b) other devices with a maximum weight of 20 kg, excluding fans and extraction or recycling hoods with built-in fans, even if filtering ($n^{\circ} 84.14$), centrifugal clothes wringers ($n^{\circ} 84.21$), dishwashing machines ($n^{\circ} 84.22$), washing machines ($n^{\circ} 84.50$), irons ($n^{\circ}s 84.20 or 84.51, depending on whether they are rollers or not), sewing machines ($n^{\circ} 84.52$), electric scissors ($n^{\circ} 84.67$), and electrothermal devices ($n^{\circ} 85.16$).
5.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ} 85.17$, the term smart phones refers to cellular network phones equipped with an operating system designed to ensure the functions of an automatic information processing machine such as downloading and simultaneous operation of multiple applications, including third-party applications, and even featuring other functionalities such as a digital camera or a navigation system.
6.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ} 85.23$:
a) the term permanent semiconductor data storage devices (“flash memory cards” or “flash electronic memory cards”, for example) refers to storage devices that have a connection plug and contain, under the same envelope, one or more flash memories (“E-PROM FLASH”, for example) in the form of integrated circuits mounted on a printed circuit board. They may include a controller in the form of an integrated circuit and discrete passive components such as capacitors and resistors;
b) the term smart cards refers to cards that contain one or more electronic integrated circuits (a microprocessor, RAM, or ROM) embedded in the mass. These cards may have contacts, magnetic stripes, or integrated antennas, but do not contain other active or passive circuit elements.
7.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ} 85.24$, the term flat display module refers to devices or apparatus designed to display information, equipped with at least one display screen, which are intended to be incorporated into articles that fall under other positions before their use. The display screens of flat display modules can be flat, curved, flexible, foldable or expandable, among others.
Flat display modules may incorporate additional elements, including those necessary for receiving video signals and distributing those signals into pixels on the screen. However, $n^{\circ} 85.24 does not include display modules equipped with components intended to convert video signals (for example, an integrated circuit for scaling, a decoder integrated circuit, or an application processor) or that have taken on the character of goods of other positions.
For the purposes of classifying flat display modules defined in this Note, $n^{\circ} 85.24 has priority over any other position of the nomenclature.
8.- Printed circuits in the sense of $n^{\circ} 85.34$ are circuits obtained by placing conductors, contacts, or other printed components (such as inductors, resistors, capacitors) on an insulating support using any printing method (inlay, electroforming, etching, etc.) or through the so-called “layer” technology, excluding any element capable of producing, rectifying, modulating, or amplifying an electrical signal (such as semiconductor elements).
The term printed circuits does not cover circuits combined with elements other than those obtained during the printing process, nor discrete resistors, capacitors, or inductors.

However, printed circuits may be equipped with unprinted connection elements.
Layer circuits (either thin or thick) containing both passive and active elements obtained through the same technological process fall under $n^{\circ} 85.42$.
9.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ} 85.36$, the term optical fiber connectors, bundles, or fiber optic cables refers to connectors that simply serve to mechanically align optical fibers end-to-end in a line system. They do not perform any other function such as amplification, regeneration, or signal modification.
10.- $n^{\circ} 85.37 does not include wireless infrared devices for remote control of television receivers and other electrical devices ($n^{\circ} 85.43$).
11.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ} 85.39, the term light-emitting diode (LED) sources covers:
a) LED modules, which are electrical light sources based on light-emitting diodes (LEDs) arranged in electrical circuits and containing electrical, mechanical, thermal, or optical elements. They may also contain discrete active or passive elements or items of $n^{\circ}s 85.36 or 85.42 for the purpose of supplying power or controlling it. LED modules do not have a base designed to be easily installed or replaced in a fixture and to allow electrical contact and mechanical fastening.
b) Lamps and tubes with LED, which are electrical light sources composed of one or more LED modules, containing other elements such as electrical, mechanical, thermal, or optical elements. They differ from LED modules by their base designed to be easily installed or replaced in a fixture and to allow electrical contact as well as mechanical fastening.
12.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ}s 85.41 and 85.42, the following are considered:
a) “1^{\circ}) Semiconductor devices are devices whose operation is based on the variation of resistivity under the influence of an electric field or semiconductor transducers.
Semiconductor devices may also include an assembly of multiple elements, even equipped with active or passive devices whose function is auxiliary.
For the purpose of this definition, semiconductor transducers are semiconductor sensors, semiconductor actuators, semiconductor resonators, and semiconductor oscillators, which are types of discrete semiconductor devices that perform an intrinsic function, capable of converting any type of physical or chemical phenomenon or action into an electrical signal or converting an electrical signal into any type of physical phenomenon or action.
All elements making up a transducer are brought together in a practically inseparable manner and may also include indissociable materials necessary for the construction or functioning of a semiconductor device.
For the purpose of this definition:

1) The term “semiconductor” means built or manufactured on a semiconductor substrate or made from semiconductor-based materials manufactured using semiconductor technology, in which the substrate or semiconductor materials play a critical and irreplaceable role in the function and performance of the transducer and whose operation is based on the semiconductor, physical, electrical, chemical, and optical properties.
2) Physical or chemical phenomena relate to phenomena such as pressure, sound waves, acceleration, vibration, movement, orientation, stress, magnetic field intensity, electric field intensity, light, radioactivity, humidity, creep, concentration of chemicals, etc.
3) Semiconductor sensors are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic or mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to detect physical or chemical quantities and convert them into electrical signals produced by resulting variations of properties or mechanical structure deformation.
4) Semiconductor actuators are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic and mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to convert electrical signals into physical movement.
5) Semiconductor resonators are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic and mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency dependent on the physical geometry of these structures in response to an external electrical signal.
6) Semiconductor oscillators are a type of semiconductor devices consisting of microelectronic or mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency dependent on the physical geometry of these structures.
$2^{\circ}$) Light-emitting diodes (LED) are semiconductor devices made from semiconductor materials that convert electrical energy into visible, infrared, or ultraviolet radiation, even when electrically connected to one another and even combined with protective diodes. The light-emitting diodes (LED) of $n^{\circ} 85.41 do not contain elements aimed at supplying power or controlling power;
b) Integrated circuits:
$1^{\circ}$) monolithic integrated circuits in which the circuit elements (diodes, transistors, resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.) are created in the mass (primarily) and the surface of a semiconductor material (for example, doped silicon, gallium arsenide, silicon-germanium, indium phosphide), forming an inseparable whole;
$2^{\circ}$) hybrid integrated circuits bringing together, in a practically inseparable manner, via interconnections or wire bonds, on the same insulating substrate (glass, ceramic, etc.) passive elements (resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.) obtained by thin or thick layer circuit technology and active elements (diodes, transistors, monolithic integrated circuits, etc.) obtained through semiconductor technology. These circuits can also include discrete components;
$3^{\circ}$) multi-chip integrated circuits consisting of two or more interconnected monolithic integrated circuits, combined in a practically inseparable manner, resting on or not on one or more insulating substrates and having or not pins, but without other active or passive circuit elements.
$4^{\circ}$) multi-component integrated circuits, which are combinations of one or more monolithic, hybrid or multi-chip integrated circuits and comprising at least one of the following components: sensors, actuators, silicon oscillators, even combined together, or components performing the functions of items falling under $n^{\circ}s 85.32, 85.33, 85.41$, or inductors that fall under $n^{\circ} 85.04$, and which are brought together in a practically inseparable manner into a single body as an integrated circuit, to form a component of the type that is used to be assembled on a printed circuit board or another support, with the connecting pins, leads, joints, pads, bumps, or discs.
For the purposes of this definition:

1. Components may be discrete, independently manufactured, and then assembled into a multi-component integrated circuit or integrated with other components.
1. The expression “on silicon” means that the component is manufactured on a silicon substrate or made of silicon-based materials or manufactured on an integrated circuit chip.
3. a) Silicon sensors are composed of microelectronic or mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to detect physical or chemical phenomena and convert them into electrical signals when variations in electrical properties or deformations of the mechanical structure occur. Physical or chemical phenomena relate to phenomena such as pressure, sound waves, acceleration, vibration, movement, orientation, stress, magnetic field intensity, electric field intensity, light, radioactivity, humidity, creep, concentration of chemicals, etc.
b) Silicon actuators consist of microelectronic and mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to convert electrical signals into physical movement.
c) Silicon resonators are components that consist of microelectronic or mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency depending on the physical geometry of these structures in response to an external input.
d) Silicon oscillators are active components consisting of microelectronic or mechanical structures created in the mass or on the surface of a semiconductor and whose function is to generate a mechanical or electrical oscillation of a predefined frequency depending on the physical geometry of these structures.
For the purposes of classification of the items defined in this Note, $n^{\circ}s 85.41 and 85.42 have priority over any other position in the Nomenclature, except for $n^{\circ} 85.23$ that might cover them due to their function."
12.- For the purpose of $n^{\circ} 85.48$, the term discarded and unusable electric and battery accumulators and electrical accumulators refers to those which have become unusable as such due to breakage, cutting, wear or other reasons, or which are not likely to be recharged.

# Sub-position notes.

1. - $n^{\circ} 8525.81 includes only television cameras, digital cameras and ultra-fast camcorders that have one or more of the following characteristics:

- recording speed greater than $0.5 \mathrm{~mm}$ per microsecond;
- temporal resolution of 50 nanoseconds or less;
- image frequency exceeding 225,000 images per second.

2- regarding $n^{\circ} 8525.82, radiation-resistant cameras are designed or shielded to operate in environments subject to high radiation levels. These cameras are designed to withstand a total radiation dose of more than $50 \times 10^{3} \mathrm{~Gy}$ (silicon) ($5 \times 10^{6} \mathrm{rad}$ (silicon)) without compromising their functionality.

3- Sub-position $8525.83 covers television cameras, digital cameras and night vision camcorders that use a photocathode to convert available natural light into electrons that can be amplified and converted to produce a visible image. This sub-position excludes thermal imaging cameras ($n^{\circ} 8525.89, generally).
4. - $n^{\circ} 8527.12 only covers radio cassettes with built-in amplifier, without built-in speaker, capable of operating without an external power source and not exceeding dimensions of 170 mm x $100 \mathrm{~mm} \times 45 \mathrm{~mm}$.
5.- For purposes of $n^{\circ}s 8549.11 to 8549.19, discarded electric batteries and accumulators refer to those that have become unusable as such due to breakage, cutting, wear, or other reasons, or those not likely to be recharged.
